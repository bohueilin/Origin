The evidence layer for AI agents

A reference check before the right to act.

Origin binds an agent configuration, challenges a policy with deterministic synthetic tasks, and produces a tamper-evident evidence artifact a reviewer can recheck.

The public demo is browser-local, synthetic, session-signed, and unpinned (signed with a key your browser makes for that run, with no published key it must match). Execution is not attempted. Not production SaaS or compliance certification.

For the team facing security review.

Agent-platform, security, and infrastructure teams need to make a specific workflow reviewable: the permissions requested, the policy decisions observed, and the limits of the evidence.

First wedge: internal-ops and production-access agent workflows. Capability alone does not establish permission.

One artifact, explicit boundaries.

  • The configuration and evaluation context are bound to the result.
  • The deterministic oracle is the sole grading authority.
  • Signature and digest checks establish integrity under their bindings; an unpinned demo signer does not establish trusted Origin issuance.
  • A synthetic pass does not establish production readiness.

Start with one blocked workflow.

Bring one action the agent would take, the tools it would touch and the approval question. After a founder-led review you receive a short written report naming the cases your reviewer would need to see and the evidence still missing. No record of your agent's decisions can be produced today; any record attached is a browser reference check of a selected example policy and rates UNTRUSTED.

45-minute review session · no charge · reply within 2 working days. Not a guarantee of approval. Send nothing confidential: no data agreement is in place yet.

Runtime gating and a controlled tool-call proxy are proposed pilot architecture, not deployed general enforcement. Real customer evidence requires separate authorization.
