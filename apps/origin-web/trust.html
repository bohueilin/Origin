01 / Current posture

What exists today. What still needs agreement.

The public prototype demonstrates an evaluation and evidence workflow. A runtime gate and controlled tool-call proxy remain proposed pilot architecture, not deployed general enforcement. Reference check vs runtime enforcement draws each step that runs today and each that is only proposed, and the verification workbench runs the checks in your browser on synthetic inputs.

Integrity is a specific claim. “Tamper-evident” means an alteration can be detected by the relevant digest, signature, binding, or chain check. It does not establish safety, production readiness, or reviewer acceptance.

04 / Responsibility

Verification informs a decision. It does not take ownership of it.

Capability is not permission. The customer retains responsibility for its systems, agent deployment, business decisions, and release authority.

01 / Evaluation

Deterministic authority

The verifier evaluates the bound checks. Model output does not grade itself. A failed integrity or configuration check invalidates the relevant artifact.

02 / Evidence

Check the artifact type

Supported artifacts carry different bindings, signatures, digests, chains, or inclusion proofs. The verifier reports the checks and limitations of the artifact you provide. You can exercise each mechanism in the verification workbench and compare held with exercised authority in the over-grant analyzer.

03 / Release

A named owner decides

A demo result does not grant production access. Integration design, access controls, incident ownership, and residual risk need separate review and agreement.
