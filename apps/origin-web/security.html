The verification workbench

Trust starts with a check you can repeat.

Sign a receipt. Change one field. Challenge a signer. Follow a policy version. Six small experiments make the evidence mechanisms visible and inspectable.

The verification code runs locally on synthetic inputs. These demonstrations establish bounded properties under their verifiers; they do not provide production runtime enforcement or certify an agent. See what runs today and what is only proposed.

  • 01 · Signature integrity — Flip one byte and it voids.
  • 02 · Batch inclusion — Eight receipts. One signature. Each checkable alone.
  • 03 · Policy history — Yesterday's decision, judged by yesterday's policy.
  • 04 · Configuration binding — Change the configuration. Re-check the evidence.
  • 05 · Delegated authority — Widen one edge. Watch the root's blast radius move.
  • 06 · Authority analysis — Inspect the authority that goes unused. Full page: the over-grant analyzer.

01 · Signature integrity

How it works
A key made in this tab signs the SHA-256 digest of a synthetic receipt. The check recomputes the digest and tests the signature against the public key carried in the attestation.
What it shows
Changing the receipt without re-signing, corrupting the signature, or signing with a key other than the pinned one (the check is given the expected key's thumbprint) fails. It does not show whose key it is.

02 · Batch inclusion

How it works
Eight synthetic receipts, each hashed with its beneficiary, fold into one Merkle root, and only the root is signed. Receipt #4 is checked with a three-hash inclusion proof.
What it shows
Changing receipt #4 or its beneficiary breaks the proof, and the check needs none of the other seven receipts. It does not show that what a receipt says is true.

03 · Policy history

How it works
Three policy versions form a hash chain: each records its parent's digest. A $120 payment decision records the digest of v2, the version it ran under.
What it shows
Rewriting a stored version without re-hashing breaks the chain, and the v2 decision fails a check against v3. Nothing signs the chain here, and the check does not test the decision against the policy's rules.

04 · Configuration binding

How it works
Deterministic code grades the Least-privilege preset's answers to 12 synthetic IAM requests against the oracle, Origin's answer key. The credential records one digest of the declared model, tools, context and harness, and another of the battery.
What it shows
How many answers match the oracle, and that the credential fails (code 4) once the declared model changes. No agent is contacted or run, and the credential is unsigned.

06 · Authority analysis

How it works
Five metrics, each with a stated denominator, over a seeded synthetic fleet (seed 20260818, 400 roots). A second seeded corpus plants widened edges, so detection is scored against known answers.
What it shows
How much granted authority went unused in the window, and whether every planted edge is found. It describes generated data, not a real fleet, and an unused scope is not by itself one to remove.
